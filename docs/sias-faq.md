## 2. Curriculum

### (a) Krea Courses Core and Skill (KCCS)

Core courses: Creative Expression (CE), Mathematical Reasoning (MR), Scientific Reasoning (SCR), Philosophical Perspectives Across Cultures (PHP), Exploring the Social and the Historical (ESH), Literature and the Arts (LAA), Engaging with the Environment (EE, offered in T9 only).

Skill courses: Writing and Oral Communication (WOC), Introduction to Computational Thinking (ICT), Data Analytics (DAN), Design Thinking (DTG), Ethics (ETS).

Each is 3 credits. In the first two trimesters they are assigned automatically; from T3 on you pick your own courses, including the remaining KCCS ones. All 12 are required to graduate.

**By when must core and skill courses be done?** The 11 offered in the first year must be completed by the end of the second year. EE is only offered in T9 and must be completed then.

**Can I withdraw from a core or skill course?** No.

### (b) Four-Year Undergraduate Programme (FYUP)

You may take a double major in any two subjects. The joint major is discontinued for batches after 2022-25.

| FYUP (Honours)                                | Credits    |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Credit limit                                  | 160 to 176 |
| Major (50% of total)                          | 80         |
| Double major (40% each)                       | 64 + 64    |
| Minor                                         | 32         |
| Concentration                                 | 16         |
| Research project (inside the major's credits) | 12         |

Core and skill courses stay at 36 credits, including EE. A 2-credit Indian language course may be added.

**How many 20-credit trimesters can I take?** Two.

**Recommended load?** Minimum 12 to stay full time. The FYUP averages about 14 a term, but take 4 courses a trimester and use the extra time in fourth year for research or a capstone.

**Maximum per trimester?** 20.

**Degree for a double major with one science major?** If at least one major is a science, a BSc Honours in four years, or a BSc if you exit in three.

### (c) Three-year exit option

You can exit with a bachelor's degree, without honours, after three years.

| Three-year exit         | Credits       |
| ----------------------- | ------------- |
| Credit limit            | 120 to 128    |
| Major (50% of total)    | 60            |
| Double major (40% each) | 48 + 48       |
| Minor                   | 24            |
| Concentration           | 16            |
| Research project        | not available |

If, and only if, you are doing a double major (48 credits each) in non-cognate disciplines and can't cross-list any course between them, you may go up to 132 credits.

**Can I do a double major in three years?** From the 2023-27 batch onward, yes. The 2021-24 and 2022-25 batches can't.

### (a) Disciplines

You can't major in Business Studies, Philosophy or Global Arts, but you can minor or take a concentration in them. Each discipline's requirements document lists its required courses and some sample electives (not exhaustive; electives come and go). Any course with that discipline's code counts toward its credits.

### (b) Major, minor, track and concentration

- **Major:** your primary specialisation. Single or double.
- **Minor:** optional, alongside the major, if you have the credit space.
- **Track:** an optional 24-credit interdisciplinary or professional programme, still being introduced.
- **Concentration:** an optional 16-credit specialisation, narrower than a minor or track.

You indicate a major preference around the second trimester of first year (a form from the Academic Office; not binding). By the end of the first trimester of second year you update your major with the Academic Office. By third year, major, minor and concentration are binding. A minor or concentration isn't mandatory, but without one you take extra courses outside your major.

**What if I don't declare a major?** You must. Otherwise you may lose priority for capped courses in that discipline.

**Can I change my major?** Yes, but it's not advisable; talk to your mentor first.

**When do I declare a minor or concentration?** Whenever you want one, through the Academic Office. Neither is required.

**Open minor?** An informal term for not having decided on a minor. The credits depend on what's left after your major and KCCS. It never appears on the transcript.

**Double minor?** Generally not possible; it would exceed the credit limit.

**How many concentrations?** As many as 160 credits, four extra courses and cross-listing allow.

### (c) Course registration

The course catalogue for each trimester lists, per course: group and course code, title, credits, eligibility, faculty, type (required or elective) and other details, grouped by division. Group codes mean a course is cross-listed, e.g. ARTS235 | ENVS237 counts as Global Arts and Environmental Studies.

Two registration forms each trimester: the initial one at the start of the sampling period (first two weeks), where you choose up to 7 to 9 courses, and the final one at the end, where you can only drop or keep what you chose.

- **Sampling / shopping period:** the first two weeks, when you can try courses before deciding. Not in T1 and T2.
- **Auditing:** taking a class without a grade or credits, if the instructor allows it. Up to 8 credits of audits across the degree. Sitting in without registering is fine with the instructor's permission.
- **Cross-listing:** one course marked under several disciplines can tick off requirements in two of your programmes, but its credits count once.
- **Capstone / research project:** a final-year project worth 12 credits toward the major, replacing 12 credits of electives.

#### First year, trimester 3

Register for four courses, at least two of them KCCS. Minimum 12 credits, maximum 15/16.

| Option | Courses                                                                | Credits        |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| I      | all three remaining core/skill courses + one course from the catalogue | 3×3 + 4 = 13   |
| II     | two core/skill courses + two from the catalogue                        | 3×2 + 4×2 = 14 |

Other combinations exist; ask your MoL representative and the Academic Office. Sections are allotted randomly. From T3 you register for every course, KCCS included. A dropped KCCS course is taken the next time it's offered, and all 11 first-year ones must be done by the end of second year. You can't go over 15 by keeping a KCCS course instead of dropping one. You can't register for only the three KCCS courses. If nothing fits your intended major, take any elective to meet the credits. If you don't register, you aren't mapped to courses on Canvas until the end of the drop period. Fill both forms.

#### Second, third and fourth years

At least 12 credits for full-time status; 16 recommended. Besides your confirmed courses you can sample 3 or 4 more. Up to 20 credits a term, but only twice. Consult your mentor and the faculty member before taking extra courses; no official approval is needed. Staying under the overall credit ceiling is your responsibility.

- A catalogue elective missing from the discipline document still counts if it carries the discipline's code.
- A course left off the initial form can't be added in the final form.
- If a course you weren't eligible for opens up, talk to the Academic Office.
- The catalogue's Details section may say which courses a course is a prerequisite for.
- Consulting your mentor before registering is recommended, not required.
- Second-years can't pick KCCS sections; the OAA sorts out clashes.
- You can stop attending a course mid-shopping period (tell the instructor) and formally drop it in the final form.
- Co-requisites (a course and its prerequisite in the same trimester) are generally not allowed. Faculty may waive a prerequisite case by case.
- Take required courses, especially for your major, when they're offered; they're often prerequisites.
- 19 credits generally doesn't count as one of your two 20-credit trimesters, though it may be restricted to keep you under the ceiling.
- Registering 20 and dropping one at the end of the shopping period: not a 20-credit trimester. Dropping one mid-trimester: still a 20-credit trimester.

#### Timetable

The timetable comes with the registration form; choose courses that fit it. For clashes, ask your MoL representative; clashes between required courses get priority. Prioritise your major's required courses, then your minor's, then electives. Attendance counts during the shopping period.

### (d) Cross-listing

A cross-listed course can tick off a requirement in each of two disciplines, but its credits count once: a 4-credit course doesn't give 8 credits, it frees 4 credits for something else. In the final year a form asks, course by course, whether to cross-list; if not, you choose which discipline it counts toward. You can only cross-list between disciplines you're doing a major, minor or concentration in.

- **How many?** Only two courses can be cross-listed under the FYUP.
- **Between the two majors of a double major?** Yes, the same as between a major and a minor.

### (e) Auditing

Auditing is for enrichment: no grade, no credits, and a workload set by the instructor. Not every course can be audited. Register an audit in consultation with your mentor and with the instructor's approval, and upload their approval email in the final form. Audit vs credit can only be switched during the shopping period. A completed audit shows as "AU" on the transcript and earns no credit.

You may audit one course per trimester, up to courses worth 8 credits across the whole programme, so how many audits you get depends on their credits.

- Sampling is the first two weeks; auditing covers the whole trimester.
- You can't audit a course without its prerequisites.
- You can't switch to auditing mid-trimester, or convert either way after week two.
- You can audit now and retake for credit later, but not the reverse.
- You can sample a course, then drop it, keep it for credit, or audit it.
- Dropping an audit late (not meeting its conditions) keeps it off the transcript but uses up an audit.
- An audited course doesn't satisfy a prerequisite; the prerequisite must be taken for credit.
- You can't fail an audit, but not meeting its conditions uses up an audit.

### (f) Capstone and independent study

- **Capstone:** each discipline shares its own requirements and timeline; ask your discipline coordinator.
- **Independent study / directed reading:** a reading-intensive course you design with a faculty member. Usually offered every trimester. One per degree, if an instructor agrees.

### (g) Attendance

At least 75% attendance, relaxable to 60–70% for documented extenuating circumstances (medical leave, representing the university). You're still marked absent; only the minimum is relaxed. File the leave application on the ERP. Missing the minimum means a failing grade, no final exam or further submissions, no supplementary exam, and a retake. Attendance counts during the shopping period; for clashes, talk to the instructor.

### (h) Withdrawing from courses

Dropping at the end of the shopping period leaves no trace on the transcript. Withdrawing after it, until the end of week six, gives a W; retaking the course later replaces the W. Not available for KCCS courses. The Academic Office shares a withdrawal form.

- A W doesn't affect your GPA.
- Auditing a course later doesn't replace a W.
- No withdrawals after week six.
- Failing the retake of a withdrawn course gives a U.

### (i) Minimum academic standards

- A probation meeting is called with a U or I in three or more courses, or a CGPA under 5.5. On probation you can't register for courses and must send fortnightly progress reports to the Academic Office.
- All first-year courses must be passed before third year, and all second-year courses before fourth year.
- Below a 3.5 CGPA you are asked to leave the university.
- The Dean decides registration appeals; the Vice Chancellor decides appeals by students asked to leave.

## 4. Exams, assessment and grades

See the Exam Office's exam manual for the details.

### (a) Grades and GPA

| Score (%) | Letter | Grade point |
| --------- | ------ | ----------- |
| 98–100    | A+     | 10          |
| 95–97     | A      | 9.6         |
| 90–94     | A-     | 9.2         |
| 85–89     | B+     | 8.7         |
| 80–84     | B      | 8.2         |
| 75–79     | B-     | 7.7         |
| 70–74     | C+     | 7.2         |
| 65–69     | C      | 6.7         |
| 60–64     | D      | 6.2         |
| 50–59     | E      | 5.5         |
| 0–49      | U      | 0           |

Marks are also turned into a Learning Trajectory Assessment (Outstanding, Excellent, Proficient, Satisfactory, Unsatisfactory), with no strict correspondence to the letter grades.

GPA = Σ (grade point × credits) / total credits, leaving out audits and pending grades. TGPA is one trimester's; CGPA is over every completed trimester. Example: B (8.2 × 4) + A- (9.2 × 4) + B+ (8.7 × 2) + A (9.6 × 4) = 125.4 over 14 credits = 8.96. A score of .5 or more rounds up (94.5% is an A).

### (b) Transcripts

Released a few weeks after each trimester, with a full-year one over the summer. The graduation transcript groups courses under major, minor, concentration and other. The Dean's List is the top 10% of a batch, noted on each year's transcript. Removing a completed course from the transcript is usually not allowed; ask the Exam Office or the Deans in extreme cases, and don't take extra courses.

### (c) Grade improvements

**Revaluation:** open for a week after grades are released (or a week after a pending grade arrives). Ask the instructor for the grade breakdown first. Only components weighted 15% or more count; quizzes and participation don't. A second evaluator grades it anonymously without seeing the first grades; late or plagiarism penalties still apply and bonus marks don't. The new grade is the average of both evaluations. Once started you can't withdraw the request.

**Supplementary and improvement exams:** improvement exams are for a D or E; supplementary exams for a U. Both need the attendance minimum (75%, or 60% with excused absences) and 40% of assignments and participation done, otherwise you retake the course. Exams are out of 60, with at least two components (assignments, exams, quizzes, viva), on the whole syllabus; the other 40% comes from your marks. The format and schedule come ten days before; there's a fee. Up to three improvement exams under the three-year exit and four under the four-year programme. The Exam Office emails each cycle's details. A B or C can't be improved. After failing, take the supplementary exam if eligible, or retake the course when it's next offered.

**Retakes:** only after a W or a U.

### (d) Incomplete and pending grades

**Incomplete (I):** if unavoidable circumstances mean you miss assignments or exams, you can ask the instructor for an Incomplete and finish within four to six weeks after exam week; then the grade is based on what you submitted. It's given only with the instructor's permission, and to the course, not an assignment. Missing the extended deadline makes you ineligible for the improvement or supplementary exam. For an Incomplete in an audit course, write to your faculty, the Academic Office, the Exam Office and the Deans.

**Pending:** the instructor hasn't submitted grades yet. The transcript updates when they do, with no lasting effect on your GPA.
